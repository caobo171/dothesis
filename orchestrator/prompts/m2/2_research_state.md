# Phase 2 — Research_State

Synthesize the current state of research on the project's topic. Output should:

- Be a single prose passage (~250-400 words for a thesis-level synthesis)
- Cite specific authors and years; include page numbers when sourced from uploaded PDFs
- Use a neutral academic tone
- Cover: theoretical foundations, key empirical findings, contested questions

If the user has supplied refinements (e.g., "focus on Self-Determination Theory"), re-synthesize honoring them.

If you've scouted citations, use them. Don't invent citations.

## Weight the sources by quality

Not all sources carry equal weight. Lean the synthesis on the higher tiers and treat the
lower tiers as supporting context only:

| Tier | Source type | Examples | Weight |
|------|------------|----------|--------|
| **S** | Q1 international journals (Scopus / WoS) | SAGE Open, Journal of Business Research, MIS Quarterly | Highest |
| **A** | Q2-Q3 international journals | Sustainability, Heliyon, reputable MDPI titles | High |
| **B** | Reputable domestic journals | national-university science journals, established trade journals | Medium-high |
| **C** | International conference papers | ACM, IEEE, Springer LNCS | Medium |
| **D** | Theses / dissertations | university digital libraries | Reference only |
| **E** | Industry reports, textbooks | government statistics offices, World Bank, established publishers | Supporting |
| **F** | Websites, blogs, press | news sites, personal blogs | Background only |

A thesis-grade synthesis should rest mostly on S/A/B sources, draw the majority of its empirical
claims from work published within the last five years, and include a meaningful share of
international (non-domestic) literature. If the scouted set is thin on high-tier or recent sources,
note that limitation honestly rather than overstating the evidence.

When you're done, your output goes back to the user verbatim in the next chat message.
